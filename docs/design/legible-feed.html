The Legible Feed

A Farcaster client built on one bet: the explanation of your feed and the control over it should be the same object.

The finding

The server already explains itself. The client files it under a menu.

Every item in the Farcaster home feed arrives with a meta block naming why it’s there — a ten-member includeReason union, a ranking score, an author quality tier. The reference client turns it into a caption in a menu, one cast at a time, with nothing to do about it.

Search the snapshot for what happens to includeReason and you find six call sites attaching it to a tracking payload, one label that gives eight of the ten reasons a sentence inside a menu, and one web-only top hat that puts two above the row. None of them can be tapped, and the most common reason of all — you follow them — gets no words anywhere. meta.score reaches one internal admin page and no reader; meta.authorQuality has zero non-type references anywhere in the repository.

That’s the wedge. Feed transparency is normally a backend project. Here it’s a rendering change against a field that’s already in the payload.

The thesis, running

The explanation is the control

Transparency without control is a shrug. So in Home they’re the same object: the chip that says Popular is the button that turns Popular down. Drag the weights and watch the feed re-rank — this runs the same normalise → weight → decay maths as the shipping kernel.

Where controls live

A settings screen is a repair shop, not a showroom

The failure mode of customizable software is a settings screen with two hundred toggles that nobody opens. The reference client is already partway there. So the organising decision in Home is where a control lives, not just what it does.

Six gestures in the feed reach roughly 80% of the surface: tap a why-chip, pull past refresh, long-press a cast, tap “N hidden”, long-press a feed tab, pinch for density. None require knowing a settings screen exists. Settings is where you go to see what you’ve accumulated and undo it.

Every automatic decision Home makes about a reader’s feed is visible, named, attributable, and reversible — from the feed itself.

Four moments

Each one takes a decision the software was making silently, names it, and hands over the handle

The why-chip names the ranker’s decision

On every cast in a ranked feed, in the metadata line. In a Following or List feed there’s nothing to explain, so no chip appears — the absence is itself information. Tap it and the sheet above opens.

It costs one render change against a field the client already receives, and it teaches the entire mental model without a word of onboarding copy: your feed is made of named parts, and you own the parts.

Receipts name the filter’s decisions

An unauditable filter is indistinguishable from a bug. Readers who suspect their client is quietly eating posts stop trusting the filters they set themselves — and then stop setting them.

The ledger is what makes aggressive muting safe enough to be worth doing. It also solves the mute-expiry problem: when rules lapse, Home surfaces them once rather than silently reopening the floodgates.

Wind-down names the decision to keep going

Past 75% of a budget, colour drains from the interface. Not a modal, not a lock — a gradient is noticeable without demanding a decision, and impossible to dismiss into meaninglessness.

Paired with catch-up mode, this is the sharpest thing Home does. Catch-up shows everything since your last visit and then ends. A feed with a bottom. The copy states a fact and never scolds.

Build order

Each milestone ships alone and convinces alone

No foundation-laying phase that pays off three milestones later. Steps one and two are the product; everything after is depth.

M1

Legibility — the feed explains itself

The adapter, why-chips, the reason sheet, the preferences provider, export and import. One render change against a field the API already sends. No backend work, no new fetch code.

Done when a reader can tap any cast, learn truthfully why it’s there, and act on the answer without leaving the feed.
M2

Control — the explanation becomes the handle

The pipeline at the flatItems seam, sift and sort, receipts, the mix dial. Ship receipts with filtering, never after.

Watch the ratio of mutes created to mutes reverted. Near-zero reversion means readers are afraid to experiment.
M3

Composition — feeds become things you make

FeedSpec-backed tabs, the feed editor with live preview, lists and searches and blends, share links, an import preview that shows what you’re accepting.

Watch how many readers open a feed someone else built. That’s the number that says taste became portable.
M4

Appearance — the palette opens

Seed themes through getTheme, the ten-role to 797-token mapping, density and font scale, per-feed skins. The largest mechanical change and the most parallelisable.

Done when a reader can build a theme they’d share, and cannot build one they can’t read.
M5

Boundaries — the app takes the reader’s side

Budgets, quiet hours, wind-down, the finite catch-up feed. Last because a session limiter on a mediocre feed reads as an apology; on a feed the reader built themselves it reads as respect.

Done when a reader can reach the end of their feed.

Status

What exists today

The design and the engine are done. packages/home-personalization implements all six axes as pure TypeScript with no dependencies — no React, no React Native, no API client, no storage engine — so it runs identically on iOS, on the web, and under vitest. 195 tests, typechecked strict.

Writing it surfaced three things worth keeping: the rank normaliser has to map onto an open interval or the lowest-scored item is immune to every weight; share links carry user-authored regular expressions, so the validator rejects quantified groups outright rather than risk a frozen feed on someone else’s phone; and ApiUserQuality is seven values, not three, with unranked exempt from any floor — a quality filter that silently hid every unrated account would mostly hide new ones.

Since then: an adapter typechecked against the reference client’s generated API types, a client core that runs the whole journey without a screen, and the feed seam and why-chip wired into a fork of the reference snapshot and proven on its compiled output. The client beyond that chip is not built yet.
